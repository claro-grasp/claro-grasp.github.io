TL;DR

CLARO is a training-free VLM agent that finds and grasps an object named in natural language, even when it is hidden in clutter. It calls perception, grasp, and push tools in a closed loop and keeps a memory of its past actions. It reaches 88.8% success on a real Franka arm and 89.1% in simulation.

Abstract

Robots that follow natural-language instructions unlock a wide range of autonomous applications in households, warehouses, and labs. Real-world environments are diverse and often heavily cluttered, requiring robots to plan over multiple steps, act, and replan as they work toward a goal. We present CLARO (CLutter-Aware Robot Orchestrator), a VLM agent that orchestrates a set of perception and manipulation skills: open-vocabulary object detection, segmentation, 6-DoF grasping, and non-prehensile pushing to locate and retrieve a language-specified target in clutter. CLARO maintains a memory of its past actions to support long-horizon execution, and emits manipulation targets (grasp poses/3D waypoints) allowing transfer across robot arms without task-specific training.

We evaluate CLARO against a range of methods on a dynamic simulation environment and standard image-based benchmarks, demonstrating strong generalization across object classes, lighting, and clutter levels. CLARO attains an average success rate of 89.1%, a relative improvement of over 16% on the strongest competing method in high cluttered scenes. We further analyze performance along grounding and grasping axes to characterize where each method succeeds and fails. Finally, we deploy CLARO on a robot arm across diverse cluttered scenes, achieving a success rate of 88.8%, and demonstrating strong sim2real generalizability.

Method

Given an RGB-D observation and an instruction, a VLM planner (Gemini 3.1 Pro) reads the current scene and a long-term memory of past images, tool calls, and plan/belief traces, and selects the next tool. Perception tools ground the scene. Manipulation tools act on it. After each action, the new observation is added to memory.

Key findings

+35.7%

Long-term memory benefits with structured outcome state, not visual replay

Without memory, high-clutter success falls by 35.7 points. Removing all past images but keeping the text record of tool results costs only 8.2 points. Keeping the images but removing the text costs 26.9 points.

+28.6%

Non-prehensile push improves robustness of open-vocabulary graspers

Removing push lowers success by 12.1 points in low clutter and by 28.6 points in high clutter.

1.9% vs 15.1%

Strong spatial grounding does not translate to reliable manipulation in clutter

From low to high clutter, grounding success falls by only 1.9 points, but grasp success falls by 15.1 points.
